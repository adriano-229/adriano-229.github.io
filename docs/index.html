Business plan proposal

This plan follows the intrapreneur guidelines. Legal steps such as registration, licenses and insurance are not included because the project would be developed inside an existing company.

DevBox

DevBox would be an internal tool for creating complete and identical development environments with one command. Each environment would include the operating system, tools, dependencies and test services, and would run as a container on the company's Kubernetes cluster.

New developers currently spend several days installing and configuring their computers. Teams also lose time solving "it works on my machine" problems. DevBox would address both issues.

The first version would include a repository with versioned environment definitions and a small Python command-line tool to create, start and delete environments. It would initially support one team's main project so that real users could test it quickly.

The main alternatives are doing nothing, using shared setup documents, or using external services such as GitHub Codespaces and Gitpod. DevBox would be different because it uses the company's own infrastructure, keeps code and data in-house, and is adapted to the company's projects.

I would ask developers, the infrastructure team and HR for feedback. I would also check whether someone was already working on a similar solution. The project would start with a short presentation to the directors and a pilot with one volunteer team. The results would be measured by comparing onboarding time before and after the pilot.

I would lead the prototype as the intrapreneur. The infrastructure team would support the cluster, and a senior developer would review the environment definitions.

Financial projection

These are illustrative estimates:

  • 20 developers, 8 new hires per year and an average cost of USD 20 per hour.
  • Reducing onboarding from three days to half a day would save about 160 hours per year.
  • Reducing environment-related problems by half would save about 500 hours per year.
  • Total estimated savings: about USD 13,200 per year.
  • First-year cost: about USD 5,800, including development and infrastructure.
  • Estimated first-year benefit: about USD 7,400, with payback in five to six months.

In the short term, the goal would be to complete the pilot in three months. In twelve months, DevBox could support all projects. In the long term, the same environments could be offered to clients and external collaborators.

The main risks are low adoption, maintenance costs and security problems in the shared cluster. These risks could be reduced by involving developers and the infrastructure team from the beginning and by keeping the first version small.

Influence Strategies I Value Most

Those are:

  1. Leading by Example: Demonstrating values and behaviors not only by words but also through actions. "A leader has to be in the midst of the troops" may summarize this concept. For example, when I teach tennis I usually highlight the fact that maintaining mental peace is really important, specially when things don't go as expected, as the opponent may take that as sign of weakness. I consider to do it myself at least when playing tennis matches! 😊

  2. Inspirational Appeal: Appealing to shared values, collective awareness, and a common sense of purpose to motivate others. I consider this a healthy way to encourage cooperation and positive change. For example, when working in group and having a deadline soon: I usually encourage the members to prevent last minute crunching by taking the necessary measures such as investing a bit of time every day, as this will be healthier and logistically better for the team.

I believe both strategies promote trust, authenticity, and mutual respect rather than relying on pressure or manipulation.

Change, how to surf the uncertainty?

One significant change in my life was deciding to leave Industrial Engineering after three years and switch to Computer Science. I had a persistent feeling that something was wrong and that I needed a different path. Still, changing careers was difficult. I worried about the uncertainty of the future and felt sad about the possibility of losing three years of effort.

Four years later, I consider the change positive. Looking back, I realize that Industrial Engineering’s career opportunities did not particularly interest me. Computer Science, by contrast, gives me more opportunities to explore research, abstract ideas, and the theoretical side of problems. Furthermore, it's important to say that the first years of both degrees have a lot in common in terms of the topics involved, almost all of them were related to the same formal sciences. Thus, it wasn't much of a waste of time in that sense, maybe the opposite.

Several ideas from the course material can explain this process. First, the change involved resistance, especially the fear of the unknown and the desire for security. Second, Lewin’s model applies to my experience. The unfreezing stage began when I recognized my dissatisfaction with my original degree. The implementation stage involved changing careers and adapting to a new field. Finally, refreezing meant settling into Computer Science and confirming over time that it was a better fit.

The Stanley 1913 Company, their Mission, Vision and Values

Stanley 1913, founded by William Stanley Jr. in 1913, is a global lifestyle brand famous for inventing the all-steel vacuum bottle. For over a century, it has delivered durable, innovative food and beverage gear.

Vision: The company's vision is to "fuel the human experience, showing up for people who show up every day—across every continent, all around the world".

Mission: Stanley's mission is to "create, build and invent in a better way, for a better life and world". This is rooted in its "Built For Life™" promise, which drives its commitment to sustainability and reducing single-use waste.

Values: Stanley's core values are durability, relevance, and tradition —a commitment to products that last a lifetime, backed by a lifetime warranty. Parent company PMI adds guiding principles of sustainability, community, teamwork, accountability, and integrity.

Personal Opinion

What draws me to Stanley is its unwavering focus on quality and durability—values that feel increasingly overlooked today. In a culture where everything is treated as disposable, I am interested in products built to last, not to be replaced.

The company's logo is a crowned bear with wings. The bear represents resilience, the wings the nature or unbelievable, and the crown is a nod to the creator, who was a famous electrical engineer.

The Cultural Organization of Rockstar Games

Rockstar Games is one of the most influential video game developers in the world, renowned for creating the Grand Theft Auto (GTA) and Red Dead Redemption franchises. Founded in 1998 by Dan and Sam Houser, the company operates as a subsidiary of Take-Two Interactive and has grown to over 4,700 employees across 12 offices worldwide. Its games are known for their sprawling open worlds, cinematic storytelling, and immense cultural impact.

The studio’s organizational culture is defined by a powerful tension between its relentless pursuit of creative excellence and a historically demanding work environment. At the heart of this is an almost obsessive commitment to secrecy—one that shapes every aspect of how the company operates, from how it hires and manages employees to the extreme security measures it deploys to protect its most valuable intellectual property.

Rockstar's approach to confidentiality is so extreme that it has drawn comparisons to Apple guarding the iPhone or Coca-Cola protecting its formula. In legal filings, the company itself has described its internal security culture as comparable to these industry giants, stating that "the standards and expectations regarding information security and privacy are exponentially beyond that of most employers". Every visitor to a Rockstar studio is required to sign a non-disclosure agreement, photography is strictly prohibited, contractors are escorted by security personnel, and personal laptops, phones, and tablets are banned from connecting to Rockstar systems.

The non-disclosure agreements themselves are legendary in their severity. According to insider reports, Rockstar employees sign lifetime NDAs, meaning that developers cannot discuss specific details of their work even a decade after leaving the company. Topics such as the proprietary RAGE game engine are considered strictly taboo. A former technical director described the company as having "an almost pathological obsession with confidentiality". This secrecy is enforced by a dedicated team of five full-time investigators and a director whose sole job is to hunt for potential leaks.

The company has taken extraordinary physical measures to prevent information from escaping. After individuals used drones to spy through office windows, Rockstar installed privacy film on its building windows. External storage devices are banned on office premises, and remote work has been heavily restricted—a controversial policy introduced after a massive 2022 data breach that saw early GTA 6 footage leaked online. Rockstar co-head Aaron Garbut described the 2022 leaks as feeling "really weirdly invasive," saying "it's like they're inside your head".

This culture of secrecy has had profound consequences for employees. In October 2025, Rockstar fired 34 workers across its UK and Canadian studios, accusing them of leaking confidential information via a private Discord server. The affected workers and the Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain (IWGB) alleged that the firings were actually an act of union-busting, as all 34 were part of a Discord group discussing working conditions and unionization. One fired employee described being "brought into a room with an HR person" and terminated for breaking an NDA that every employee must sign before working there, before being immediately escorted out by security. The union characterized the firings as having "a chilling effect" on the industry. Rockstar has denied these claims, maintaining that the workers were dismissed for gross misconduct related to leaking company secrets.

The gap between Rockstar's public narrative of creative excellence and the lived experiences of many employees reveals a culture caught between its artistic ambitions and the human cost of achieving them—a culture where silence is not just encouraged, but contractually enforced for life.
