Change, how to surf the uncertainty?

One significant change in my life was deciding to leave Industrial Engineering after three years of classes and switch to Computer Science. I had a persistent feeling that something was wrong and that I needed to take a different path. I worried about the uncertainty of the future and felt sad about the possibility of losing three years of effort.

Four years later, I consider the change positive. Looking back, I realize that Industrial Engineering’s career opportunities did not particularly interest me. Computer Science, by contrast, gives me more opportunities to explore research, abstract ideas, and the theoretical side of problems, which I've allways been keen on. Furthermore, it's important to say that the first years of both degrees have a lot in common in terms of the topics involved, almost all of them were related to the same formal sciences. Thus, it wasn't much of a waste of time in that sense, maybe the opposite.

Several ideas from the course material can be explained by this process. First, the change involved resistance, especially the fear of the unknown and the desire for security. Second, Lewin’s model applies to my experience. The unfreezing stage began when I recognized my dissatisfaction with my original degree. The implementation stage involved changing careers and adapting to a new field. Finally, refreezing meant settling into the new course and confirming over time that it was a better fit.
