---
title: Change, how to surf the uncertainty?
date: 2026-10-14
---

One significant change in my life was deciding to leave Industrial Engineering after three years and switch to Computer
Science. I had a persistent feeling that something was
wrong and that I needed a different path. Still, changing careers was difficult. I worried about the uncertainty of the
future and felt sad about the possibility of losing three years of effort.

Four years later, I consider the change positive. Looking back, I realize that Industrial Engineering’s career
opportunities did not particularly interest me. Computer Science, by contrast, gives me more opportunities to explore
research, abstract ideas, and the theoretical side of problems. Furthermore, it's important to say that the first years
of both
degrees have a lot in common in terms of the topics involved, almost all of them were related to the same formal
sciences. Thus, it wasn't much
of a waste of time in that sense, maybe the opposite.

Several ideas from the course material can explain this process. First, the change involved resistance, especially the
fear of the unknown and the desire for security. Second, Lewin’s model applies to my experience. The unfreezing stage
began when I recognized my dissatisfaction with my original degree. The implementation stage involved changing careers
and adapting to a new field. Finally, refreezing meant settling into Computer Science and confirming over time that it
was a better fit.
