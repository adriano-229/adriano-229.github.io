---
title: The Cultural Organization of Rockstar Games
date: 2026-10-10
---

Rockstar Games is one of the most influential video game developers in the world, renowned for creating the *Grand Theft
Auto* (GTA) and *Red Dead Redemption* franchises. Founded in 1998 by Dan and Sam Houser, the company operates as a
subsidiary of Take-Two Interactive and has grown to over 4,700 employees across 12 offices worldwide. Its games are
known for their sprawling open worlds, cinematic storytelling, and immense cultural impact.

The studio’s organizational culture is defined by a powerful tension between its relentless pursuit of creative
excellence and a historically demanding work environment. At the heart of this is an almost obsessive commitment to
secrecy—one that shapes every aspect of how the company operates, from how it hires and manages employees to the extreme
security measures it deploys to protect its most valuable intellectual property.

Rockstar's approach to confidentiality is so extreme that it has drawn comparisons to Apple guarding the iPhone or
Coca-Cola protecting its formula. In legal filings, the company itself has described its internal security culture as
comparable to these industry giants, stating that "the standards and expectations regarding information security and
privacy are exponentially beyond that of most employers". Every visitor to a Rockstar studio is required to sign a
non-disclosure agreement, photography is strictly prohibited, contractors are escorted by security personnel, and
personal laptops, phones, and tablets are banned from connecting to Rockstar systems.

The non-disclosure agreements themselves are legendary in their severity. According to insider reports, Rockstar
employees sign lifetime NDAs, meaning that developers cannot discuss specific details of their work even a decade
after leaving the company. Topics such as the proprietary RAGE game engine are considered strictly taboo. A former
technical director described the company as having "an almost pathological obsession with confidentiality". This secrecy
is enforced by a dedicated team of five full-time investigators and a director whose sole job is to hunt for potential
leaks.

The company has taken extraordinary physical measures to prevent information from escaping. After individuals used
drones to spy through office windows, Rockstar installed privacy film on its building windows. External storage devices
are banned on office premises, and remote work has been heavily restricted—a controversial policy introduced after a
massive 2022 data breach that saw early GTA 6 footage leaked online. Rockstar co-head Aaron Garbut described the 2022
leaks as feeling "really weirdly invasive," saying "it's like they're inside your head".

This culture of secrecy has had profound consequences for employees. In October 2025, Rockstar fired 34 workers across
its UK and Canadian studios, accusing them of leaking confidential information via a private Discord server. The
affected workers and the Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain (IWGB) alleged that the firings were actually an
act of union-busting, as all 34 were part of a Discord group discussing working conditions and unionization. One fired
employee described being "brought into a room with an HR person" and terminated for breaking an NDA that every employee
must sign before working there, before being immediately escorted out by security. The union characterized the firings
as having "a chilling effect" on the industry. Rockstar has denied these claims, maintaining that the workers were
dismissed for gross misconduct related to leaking company secrets.

The gap between Rockstar's public narrative of creative excellence and the lived experiences of many employees reveals a
culture caught between its artistic ambitions and the human cost of achieving them—a culture where silence is not just
encouraged, but contractually enforced for life.
